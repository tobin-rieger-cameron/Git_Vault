# What are the Formal Sciences?
The formal sciences are branches of study that focus on systematic observation, experimentation, and logical reasoning to develop and test theories and models. They often rely on abstract concepts, axioms, and formal systems to describe and analyze phenomena.

## Key Concepts
### Mathematics
Mathematics is a classic example of a formal science, as it deals with abstract concepts such as numbers, shapes, and patterns using logical and symbolic representations. It provides a rigorous framework for describing and analyzing mathematical structures, making it a fundamental tool in many fields.

### Logic
Logic is also a formal science that deals with reasoning, inference, and argumentation. It involves the study of logical systems, including propositional and predicate logic, which are used to evaluate arguments and make deductive conclusions.

### Statistics
Statistics is a field that applies mathematical and computational techniques to analyze and interpret data. While it draws heavily from mathematics and computer science, statistics is distinct in its focus on empirical research and the application of statistical methods to understand phenomena in fields such as physics, biology, and economics.

### Computer Science
Computer science, as a formal science, focuses on designing, building, and analyzing algorithms, software systems, and other digital technologies. It relies heavily on mathematical and computational techniques to solve complex problems and develop innovative solutions.

## Key Differences from Empirical Sciences
While the formal sciences rely on abstract concepts and logical reasoning, empirical sciences focus more heavily on observation, experimentation, and data collection. Empirical sciences, such as physics, biology, or economics, often use mathematical models and statistical analysis to describe and analyze phenomena, but they differ in their approach and methodology from the formal sciences.
