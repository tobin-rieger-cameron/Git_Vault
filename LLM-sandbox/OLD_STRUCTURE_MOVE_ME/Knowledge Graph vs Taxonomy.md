# Knowledge Graph vs Taxonomy

A knowledge graph is a data structure that represents complex relationships between entities, concepts, or objects in a structured and organized way. Unlike taxonomy, which focuses primarily on categorization and hierarchy, a knowledge graph encompasses not only categorization but also the complex relationships between entities.

## Key Concepts

### What is Taxonomy?

Taxonomy is a widely used method for categorizing objects or concepts based on their relationships and characteristics. It relies on the concept of hierarchy and categorization to group living organisms in biology and organize human knowledge into meaningful categories.

### Hierarchy and Categorization

Taxonomy uses a hierarchical structure to organize knowledge, which can be seen in both biological taxonomy (e.g., the Linnaean classification system) and classification systems in knowledge organization (e.g., Dewey Decimal Classification System). However, this approach has its limitations when dealing with complex relationships between entities.

### What is a Knowledge Graph?

A knowledge graph is an enormous web of interconnected nodes, where each node represents an entity, concept, or object, and the edges represent relationships between them. Unlike taxonomy, which uses a hierarchical structure, a knowledge graph can represent relationships in a more flexible and scalable way using directed edges between nodes.

## Differences Between Taxonomy and Knowledge Graph

One key difference between taxonomy and knowledge graph is their representation style. Taxonomy typically uses a hierarchical structure, whereas a knowledge graph can represent complex relationships between entities using directed edges between nodes. This allows for more accurate modeling of semantic networks or ontologies.

Another significant distinction lies in the level of granularity and expressiveness they offer. Taxonomy is often limited to categorical relationships, whereas knowledge graphs enable the representation of nuanced relationships, including semantic links, synonyms, antonyms, and even temporal or spatial dependencies between entities.
