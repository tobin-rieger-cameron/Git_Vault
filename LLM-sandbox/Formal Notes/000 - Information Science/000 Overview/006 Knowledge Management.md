# Knowledge management

Knowledge management is the organizational practice of capturing, sharing, and making use of knowledge, with particular attention to knowledge that has not been written down anywhere — the expertise a skilled employee carries but has never documented, or the lessons a team learned informally while solving a problem. Where much of information science assumes information already exists as a recorded document, knowledge management often starts a step earlier, with knowledge that exists only in someone's head.

This makes knowledge management as much about people and incentives as about systems. Getting someone to document their expertise, or to share it through mentorship, a community of practice, or an exit interview before they leave a role, is a problem of culture and motivation, not just of building the right database. The technical tools knowledge organization and information storage provide are only useful once that first, harder step of surfacing the knowledge has happened.

Because of this emphasis on organizational behavior, knowledge management sits closer to management science than to the more document-centric divisions of information science, and its methods are drawn as much from that field as from library and information science traditions.
