# Information storage

Information storage is the study of how information is represented and physically or digitally retained so that it can be recovered later without loss or corruption. It sits between the moment information is captured and the moment it is retrieved, and much of its history is the history of that gap shrinking — from clay tablets and manuscripts, to microfilm, to the databases and file systems that hold most recorded information today.

A storage system has to solve two problems at once: how to represent information in a form suited to the medium holding it, and how to keep that representation stable and accessible over time. The two problems interact — a representation optimized for compact storage may be slow to search, while one optimized for fast retrieval may waste space — and most real systems are a negotiated compromise between the two.

Because nearly every other activity in information science assumes that information has first been captured somewhere, information storage functions as a kind of substrate for the rest of the field: a change in how storage works, such as the move from physical archives to networked databases, tends to reshape retrieval, organization, and policy in turn.
