# Informetrics

Informetrics is the quantitative study of information — how it is produced, distributed, and used, measured through methods borrowed from statistics. It grew out of bibliometrics, the measurement of publications and citations, and has since broadened to cover any recorded information that can be counted and analyzed, including web content and social media activity.

Much of informetrics traces the flow of influence between works, most commonly through citation analysis, which uses the pattern of references between publications to identify influential authors, track how ideas spread, and map the structure of entire research fields. Applied to the web specifically, these same methods — often called webometrics — analyze hyperlinks, page views, and other digital traces in place of citations.

Alongside information retrieval, informetrics is one of the two subareas widely recognized as most empirically distinct within information science, with its own methods and its own body of specialized research largely separate from the rest of the field. Its results are also put to direct practical use, most visibly in the citation counts and impact metrics used to evaluate academic research.
