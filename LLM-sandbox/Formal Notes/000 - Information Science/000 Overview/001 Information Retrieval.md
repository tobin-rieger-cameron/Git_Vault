Information retrieval is the study of how to find material that satisfies an information need within a large collection of documents or data. It covers the systems that carry this out — from library catalogs to web search engines — as well as the underlying theory of how to judge whether a given result is actually relevant to what someone was looking for.

A retrieval system typically works by matching a query against an index built from the collection, then ranking the matches by estimated relevance rather than returning them in the order found. That ranking step is where most of the field's hard problems live: the same words can mean different things in different contexts, a document can be relevant without sharing any words with the query, and what counts as a good result depends on the person asking as much as on the document itself.

Because search is the most visible way most people encounter information science in daily life, information retrieval has grown into one of the field's two most distinct and heavily studied subareas, alongside informetrics. Its methods now extend well past text search, into recommendation systems, question answering, and retrieval-augmented generation in modern AI systems.
