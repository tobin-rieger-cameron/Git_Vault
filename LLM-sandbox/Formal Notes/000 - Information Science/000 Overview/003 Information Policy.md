# Information policy

Information policy is the study of the laws, regulations, and institutional norms that govern how information is created, accessed, shared, and restricted within a society. It covers ground as varied as copyright and intellectual property, freedom of information and government transparency, data privacy, and the economics of who profits from access to information and who is excluded from it.

Unlike divisions of information science concerned with how information is organized or retrieved, information policy is concerned with who is allowed to do those things, under what conditions, and at what cost. A search engine's ranking algorithm is a retrieval question; whether that same engine can legally index a copyrighted book, or must comply with a government's data-localization law, is a policy question layered on top of it.

Because information now moves through commercial platforms as much as through libraries and archives, information policy increasingly overlaps with economics and law, and questions once confined to librarians and archivists — who owns a record, who may see it, how long it must be kept — now shape entire industries built on data.
