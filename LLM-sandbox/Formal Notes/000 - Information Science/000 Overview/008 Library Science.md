# Library science

Library science is the study of how libraries acquire, organize, preserve, and provide access to collections of recorded knowledge, along with the professional practices — cataloging, collection development, reference service, and library administration — built around those tasks. It is the oldest institutional home of information science, and many of the field's core methods, including its major classification systems, originated within it.

A library's central task is reconciling two competing goals: building a collection worth having, and making that collection findable once it exists. This has historically meant balancing physical constraints — shelf space, preservation conditions, budget — against the needs of the people the library serves, whether a small community, a university, or a national population, and the profession has developed distinct traditions of practice around each type of institution.

As recorded knowledge has shifted from physical to digital formats, much of library science's traditional territory — cataloging, retrieval, metadata — has grown into its own specialized divisions elsewhere in information science. What remains distinctly library science is the institutional and professional core: the practices, ethics, and administration of the organizations built specifically to hold and share knowledge on behalf of others.
