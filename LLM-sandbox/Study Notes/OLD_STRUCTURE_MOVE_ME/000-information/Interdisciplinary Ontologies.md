# Interdisciplinary Fields
## Category: Taxonomy
## Tags: #KnowledgeTaxonomy, #InterdisciplinaryFields

### What are Interdisciplinary Fields?
Interdisciplinary fields refer to areas of study that combine concepts, methods, or principles from two or more distinct disciplines to address complex problems or phenomena. These fields often blend boundaries between traditional subjects, creating new areas of inquiry.

### Understanding Interdisciplinary Fields
In the context of a knowledge [[Taxonomy]], interdisciplinary fields can be categorized into three main groups: Hybrid categories, Subfields of existing categories, and New top-level branches.

### Hybrid Categories

* **Cognitive Science**: Combines psychology, computer science, and philosophy to study mental processes and intelligence.
* **Bioinformatics**: Merges biology, computer science, and mathematics to analyze biological data and understand its implications for medicine and other sciences.

These hybrid categories reflect the combining disciplines that define them. They often require a deep understanding of multiple subjects to tackle complex problems.

### Subfields of Existing Categories

The humanities might include a subfield like **Computational Humanities**, which applies computational methods to humanities research, such as natural language processing for literary analysis or digital humanities for historical research. Similarly, existing categories can be subdivided into new subfields that accommodate interdisciplinary approaches.

### New Top-Level Branches

A field of study that combines social sciences, philosophy, and computer science might be grouped under a new branch called **Digital Ethics**, exploring the intersection of technology, human behavior, and moral principles.

Creating entirely new top-level branches in the taxonomy can help accommodate emerging interdisciplinary fields. However, this approach raises questions about how to organize and categorize knowledge across multiple disciplines.

### Challenges in Taxonomizing Human Knowledge
Organizing all human knowledge into categories is a daunting task due to its complexity and diversity. No universally accepted taxonomy exists for human knowledge, making it challenging to create a one-size-fits-all system.
