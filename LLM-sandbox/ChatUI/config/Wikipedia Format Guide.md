
The Wikipedia [[Wikipedia_Manual_of_Style.pdf|MoS]] provides standards on how to format articles.

Articles should be written using straightforward, succinct, and easily understood language. Articles should be structured with consistent, reader-friendly layouts and formatting. An article should start with a simple summary of the topic, then lead the reader into more detail. Breaking up the text into manageable sections with logical headings.

## Article titles, sections, and headings
---
### The lead
The very first part of an article, appearing before the table of contents or any headings, should contain a concise definition, and summary of the topic. The overview should only be *one to four paragraphs* long, and should be written in a clear, accessible style to encourage a reading of the full article. 

### Article titles
A title should be a recognizable name or description of the topic, being natural, sufficiently precise, concise, and consistent with related articles.

- Capitalize the initial letter, but otherwise follow sentence case, not title case, except where title case would be used in ordinary prose.
- Do not use *a, an, or the* as the first word, unless it is an inseparable part of the name (i.e. A Clockwork Orange).
- Use nouns or noun phrases


### Sections and headings
After the [[Wikipedia Format Guide#The lead|lead]], articles are organised into sections and subsections, each with their own short heading that will appear in the table of contents. In general, short sections (one to four paragraphs) are the most readable.

Each section is labelled by a heading; typically only the first word of a heading is capitalized. **H1** is the article's title. The section headings in the article start at **H2** with subsections at **H3**. Section headings should not skip levels.

**H2** should be followed by triple dashes `---` on the next line.

Section headings should not:
- redundantly refer back to the subject of the article
- refer to a higher-level heading
- be numbered or lettered
- be phrased as a question, unless being framed as a "How to:" article

### Ending an article
several kinds of material may appear after the main body of an article, in the following order:
- Books or other works created by the subject of the article, under the heading "Works"
- Internal links to related articles, with the section heading "See also" — though these should only be included if no inline hyperlink can be fit in context.
- References
- Relevant books, articles, or other publications that have not been used as references; under the section "Further reading"
- Relevant and appropriate websites that have not been used as sources and do not appear in the earlier appendices, using the heading "External links"

## Consistency
---
The [[Wikipedia_Manual_of_Style.pdf|MoS]] contains extensive guidelines on all manner of stylistic points. Here are some samples:

### Language
Avoid words like *I*, *we*, and *you*, except in quotations and names of works.

Avoid phrases like *note that* and *remember that* (which assume "you" for the reader); avoid such expressions as *of course*, *in fact*, and *obviously*

### Dates and numbers
Avoid phrases that will go out of date with time (e.g. recently).

Do not write #1; number one works instead.

Use commas and decimal points for thousand separators and decimal numbers respectively: 12,00 / 3.14

Both *10 June 1921* and *June 10, 1921*, are correct, but should be consistent within an article. A comma is not used if only the month is given, such as June 1921. Avoid inserting *"the year"* before a year, and avoid *"of"* in items such as "April of 2008".

*400 AD* and *400 BC* are correct, but so are *400 CE* and *400 BCE*. Use style consistently within an article.

Use *one, two, three, ..., eight, nine* in normal article text rather than **1, 2, 3, ..., 8, 9** (though there are some exceptional circumstances)

### Capital letters
Seasons and plant/animal names are *not* capitalized. Exceptions include scientific names *(Felis catus)* and proper nouns occurring as part of a name.

Names of scriptures are capitalized (e.g. *Bible* and *Qur'an*, but not *biblical*). Always capitalize *God* when referring to a primary or only deity.

### Punctuation
Italicize names of *books*, *films*, *TV series*, *music albums*, *paintings*, and *ships* -- but not short words like "songs" or "poems", which should be in quotation marks.

Place a full stop. or a comma, before a closing quotation mark if it belongs as part of the quoted material (She said, "I'm, feeling carefree."); otherwise, put it after (The word carefree means "happy".). 

The serial comma (for example the comma before and in "ham, chips, and eggs") is optional; be sensitive to possible ambiguity arising from thoughtless use/avoidance.

Avoid comma splices (joining two independent clauses).

A hyphen is not a dash. Hyphens are used within words or to join words, but not in punctuating the parts of a sentence. Use the en dash `–` with space before and after - or use an em dash (—) without spaces. Avoid using two hyphens (--) and avoid using a hyphen as a minus sign.

### Non-breaking spaces
Line breaks between words can be prevented by inserting a *non-breaking space* instead of an ordinary space. This avoids breaking in the middle of expressions.
