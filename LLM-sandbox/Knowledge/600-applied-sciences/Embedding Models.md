## Introduction

Embedding models are a crucial component of natural language processing (NLP) that transform words or phrases into numerical vectors capturing their semantic meaning. These vector representations enable various NLP tasks such as text classification, sentiment analysis, and information retrieval.

## Key Concepts

### Word Embeddings

Word embeddings represent each word as a dense vector in a high-dimensional space (usually around 300-1000 dimensions). This approach allows for the capture of semantic relationships between words, enabling more accurate predictions and generalization to unseen data.

Two popular methods used to create word embeddings are:

1. **Continuous Bag-of-Words (CBOW)**: Predicts a target word based on its context.
2. **Skip-Gram**: Predicts the context words given a target word.

### Optimization Objectives

Embedding models can be trained using various optimization objectives, such as maximizing the probability of correct word prediction or minimizing the negative log-likelihood of incorrect predictions.

## Desirable Properties of Good Embedding Models

A good embedding model typically has several desirable properties:

1. **Compactness**: The vector space should be compact enough to fit into memory and computationally efficient.
2. **Interpretability**: The vectors should capture meaningful semantic relationships between words, such as synonyms, antonyms, or related concepts.
3. **Generalization**: The model should generalize well to unseen words and contexts, reducing the risk of overfitting.

## Popular Embedding Models

Some popular embedding models include:

1. **BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers)**: A pre-trained language model that uses a multi-layer bidirectional transformer encoder to generate contextualized word embeddings.
2. **RoBERTa**: An improved version of BERT with larger training datasets and more sophisticated optimization techniques.
